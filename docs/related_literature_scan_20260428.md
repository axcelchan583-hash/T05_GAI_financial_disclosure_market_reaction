# Related Literature Scan: GenAI in Financial Reporting and Disclosure Processing

Date: 2026-04-28

## Scope

This scan focuses on literature related to Blankespoor, deHaan, and Li, "Generative AI in Financial Reporting" and the T05 project's focal construct: GenAI-assisted financial-disclosure writing.

The project originally separated disclosure-writing production from adjacent topics such as corporate AI capability, AI announcements, AI washing, and supply-chain spillovers. After the 2026-05-11 design update, GenAI announcements are reintroduced as a treatment-timing source, not as the final outcome literature.

## Directly Related to the T05 X

### Blankespoor, deHaan, and Li, 2026

- Title: Generative AI in Financial Reporting
- Outlet: Journal of Accounting Research
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/1475-679x.70050
- Earlier SSRN DOI: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4986017
- Fit: core anchor
- Key point: validates GPTZero in financial disclosures and documents detectable GenAI use in risk factors, MD&A, earnings press releases, conference-call prepared remarks, and IPO filings through 2024.
- T05 implication: establishes that GenAI-assisted disclosure writing is real and measurable, but leaves room for capital-market consequences and China A-share evidence.

### Irons, 2024/2025

- Title: The Influence of AI-Like Text on Responses to Disclosure: Evidence from AI Detection Models
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4931744
- SSRN: https://ssrn.com/abstract=4931744
- Fit: closest direct competitor after Blankespoor-deHaan-Li
- Key point: constructs AI-like text measures in earnings conference calls using commercial AI detectors; finds increased AI-like text in prepared remarks after GPT-4.0, especially among smaller/younger firms; tone in non-AI-like text is more strongly associated with returns.
- T05 implication: strong warning that market reactions to AI-like disclosure may be muted, and that conference-call evidence is already being studied.

### Choi and Xie, 2026

- Title: Human + AI in Accounting: Early Evidence from the Field
- Outlet: Journal of Accounting Research
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/1475-679x.70052
- Fit: adjacent production-function evidence
- Key point: uses survey, proprietary field data, and a framed field experiment to study GenAI adoption in accounting workflows, task allocation, productivity, and reporting quality.
- T05 implication: supports the claim that GenAI changes accounting/financial-reporting production, but the setting is accounting workflow rather than public disclosure text.

### Liang et al., 2025

- Title: The Widespread Adoption of Large Language Model-Assisted Writing Across Society
- Outlet: Patterns
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2025.101366
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09747
- Fit: broad AI-writing adoption benchmark
- Key point: estimates LLM-assisted writing across consumer complaints, corporate press releases, job postings, and UN press releases; corporate press releases reach up to about 24% LLM-attributable text by late 2024.
- T05 implication: useful for motivating AI-assisted writing in corporate communications, but not financial-reporting-specific.

## GenAI and Disclosure/Market Information Processing

### Bertomeu, Lin, Liu, and Ni, 2025

- Title: The Impact of Generative AI on Information Processing: Evidence from the Ban of ChatGPT in Italy
- Outlet: Journal of Accounting and Economics
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacceco.2025.101782
- Fit: core processing-cost evidence
- Key point: uses Italy's temporary ChatGPT ban to show GenAI affects analyst information processing; the ban reduces analyst AI usage, forecast issuance, forecast accuracy, and information efficiency.
- T05 implication: strong support for the information-processing channel, especially analyst outcomes.

### Cheng, Lin, and Zhao, 2025

- Title: Does Generative AI Facilitate Investor Trading? Early Evidence from ChatGPT Outages
- Outlet: Journal of Accounting and Economics
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacceco.2025.101821
- Fit: investor processing evidence
- Key point: ChatGPT outages reduce stock trading volume, especially around recent corporate news, consistent with investors relying on GenAI for information processing.
- T05 implication: supports annual-report disclosure outcomes tied to investor attention, trading, and price efficiency.

### Blankespoor, Croom, and Grant, 2024/2026

- Title: Generative AI and Investor Processing of Financial Information
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.5053905
- SSRN: https://ssrn.com/abstract=5053905
- Fit: retail-investor adoption/descriptive evidence
- Key point: documents how retail investors use GenAI to interpret financial information and market movements, using brokerage chatbot queries and survey evidence.
- T05 implication: supports the claim that investors actually use GenAI to process disclosures, not only hypothetically.

### Croom, 2025/2026

- Title: Interactivity and Illusions of Ability: How Using Generative AI Affects Investor Judgments
- Outlet: Journal of Accounting Research, forthcoming
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/1475-679X.70017
- Fit: experimental investor-judgment caution
- Key point: GenAI interactivity can inflate investors' self-assessed ability and willingness to invest without improving actual information processing.
- T05 implication: credibility/overconfidence channel; GenAI may not simply improve processing.

### Croom, Gale, and Grant, 2025

- Title: Disclosure Presentation Attributes, Generative AI Output, and Investor Judgments
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.5040309
- Fit: disclosure attributes + GenAI processing
- Key point: processing-cost attributes matter less for investor judgments when GenAI is present, especially for less experienced investors; contextual cues still matter.
- T05 implication: useful for mechanism framing: GenAI may weaken the role of readability/complexity frictions.

### Ecker, Li, Li, and Wu, 2026

- Title: How Stock Market Participants Use Generative Artificial Intelligence: Evidence from User-Platform Interaction Data
- Outlet: Journal of Accounting Research
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/1475-679x.70051
- Earlier SSRN DOI: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.5224596
- Fit: especially useful because it uses China platform data
- Key point: analyzes 1.7 million stock-related queries from a large Chinese GenAI platform; queries rise around disclosure events but largely track media coverage; evidence suggests substitution between voluntary disclosure informativeness and GenAI usage.
- T05 implication: directly useful for China-based investor information-processing framing.

## GenAI as Research/Measurement Tool in Accounting Text

### de Kok, 2025

- Title: ChatGPT for Textual Analysis? How to Use Generative LLMs in Accounting Research
- Outlet: Management Science
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2023.03253
- Fit: method foundation
- Key point: framework for using generative LLMs in accounting textual analysis, including construct validity, model choice, prompt design, bias, replicability, and data sharing.
- T05 implication: useful for defending a Chinese GAIWritingScore workflow that does not rely on one black-box detector.

### Bernard, Blankespoor, de Kok, and Toynbee, 2026

- Title: Using GPT to Measure Business Complexity
- Outlet: The Accounting Review
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.2308/TAR-2023-0716
- SSRN: https://ssrn.com/abstract=4480309
- Fit: method and exposure-measure support
- Key point: constructs a GPT-based measure of business complexity using financial-reporting text and iXBRL tags; complexity is associated with slower price formation.
- T05 implication: supports using pre-period reporting complexity/writing cost as exposure.

### Dugan et al., 2024

- Title: RAID: A Shared Benchmark for Robust Evaluation of Machine-Generated Text Detectors
- Outlet: ACL 2024
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.acl-long.674
- Fit: detector validation caution
- Key point: shows machine-generated-text detectors are vulnerable to domain shifts, adversarial attacks, sampling strategies, and unseen models.
- T05 implication: do not rely on a single detector; build validation, multiple measures, or disclosure-domain calibration.

### Adam et al., 2026

- Title: GPTZero: Robust Detection of LLM-Generated Texts
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13042
- Fit: detector background
- Key point: technical description of GPTZero's industrial AI-text-detection system and robustness claims.
- T05 implication: useful background because Blankespoor-deHaan-Li uses GPTZero, but not a substitute for Chinese financial-disclosure validation.

## GenAI in Corporate/Intermediary Communications

### Qian, Peng, and Li, 2025

- Title: The Impact of Generative AI Announcements on Suppliers: Evidence From the Stock Market
- Outlet: Production and Operations Management
- Local PDF: `/Users/mac/computerscience/23选题探索/bib/AI文本文献/The Impact of Generative AI Announcements on Suppliers_ Evidence From the Stock Market.pdf`
- Fit: method benchmark for GenAI announcement event construction
- Key point: constructs a firm-level GenAI announcement sample through broad keyword search, manual screening, retention of specific GenAI initiatives, first-event selection, event-date alignment, and removal of confounding/overlapping events for stock-market tests.
- T05 implication: useful for building `FirstSpecificGenAIAnnouncement` and `TreatedGenAIAnnouncement`; not a reason to copy the supplier stock-reaction Y.

### Jia, Li, Ma, and Xu, 2025

- Title: Corporate Responses to Generative AI: Early Evidence from Conference Calls
- Outlet: Review of Accounting Studies, forthcoming
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4736295
- Fit: adjacent, not the T05 X
- Key point: measures managerial and analyst discussions of GenAI in conference calls and finds they inform investors.
- T05 implication: useful related literature on GenAI discourse, but the X is talking about GenAI, not using GenAI to write disclosure.

### Bradshaw, Ma, Yost, and Zou, 2025

- Title: Generative AI Use by Capital Market Information Intermediaries: Evidence from Seeking Alpha
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.5226562
- Fit: intermediary production evidence
- Key point: AI-generated Seeking Alpha articles rise after ChatGPT, increase author productivity and firm coverage, but are less informative on average than human articles.
- T05 implication: useful analogy for low-cost AI-generated financial communication, especially the "more coverage but lower informativeness" tradeoff.

### Christ, Kim, and Yip, 2025

- Title: Word on the Street: Field Evidence of Artificial Intelligence Use in Sell-Side Equity Research
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.5071836
- Fit: intermediary-use field evidence
- Key point: sell-side analysts use AI mainly for efficiency rather than unique insights; relational networks remain important for differentiated information.
- T05 implication: supports analyst-side GenAI adoption and suggests outcome effects may depend on whether AI changes insight generation or only drafting efficiency.

## Bottom Line for T05

The first paper is not isolated anymore, but the narrow lane is still not fully saturated.

Direct competition exists mainly on US disclosures and conference calls:

1. Blankespoor-deHaan-Li: detects GenAI in multiple financial-reporting disclosures.
2. Irons: detects AI-like text in conference-call prepared remarks and studies market response.
3. Choi-Xie: field evidence on GenAI in accounting workflows.

The remaining literature mostly studies GenAI as an information-processing tool for investors, analysts, intermediaries, or supply-chain counterparties. That is useful for mechanism, treatment construction, and secondary outcomes, but it does not directly preempt a China A-share design where the target mechanism is GAI-assisted annual-report disclosure writing.

The T05 novelty should therefore be framed as:

> Do firms with specific GenAI initiative announcements subsequently produce more or less verifiable annual-report risk disclosure in the China A-share setting?

The highest-risk overlap is still "AI-like text in disclosure + market response." The supplier-announcement paper is mainly a data-processing template for constructing clean GenAI events. Any T05 design should explicitly differentiate from Irons and Chen-Wang-style disclosure-market papers by using annual reports/risk sections, China A-share data, validated Chinese text measurement, and disclosure-specific Y rather than only conference-call return response or generic market reaction.
